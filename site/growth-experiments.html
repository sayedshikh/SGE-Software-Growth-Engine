Growth experiment & optimization engine

A repeatable system for evidence-led growth experiments. It helps prioritize, define, run, record and decide—without turning assumptions into facts.

Experiment workflow

Discover

Identify a problem, friction, audience need, channel gap or measurable opportunity. Attach source evidence and date.

Hypothesize

State a falsifiable hypothesis, target segment, change, expected mechanism and uncertainty.

Design

Define one primary metric, guardrails, baseline, window, effort, budget cap and success/stop criteria.

Run

Execute only approved changes. No automatic posting, purchases, outreach or irreversible changes.

Analyze

Compare observed data with the baseline; note sample size, data quality, confounders and limitations.

Decide

Continue, modify, stop or inconclusive. Capture learning and next action, not just the outcome.

Prioritization model

Score each factor from 1–5. These are decision heuristics, not forecasts.

  • Impact: potential relevance to an important business outcome.
  • Confidence: strength of evidence supporting the hypothesis.
  • Effort: estimated time/complexity (1 low, 5 high).
  • Risk: privacy, platform, reputational, financial or product risk; separately review rather than hiding it in a score.

Priority score = Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. Review high-risk experiments separately regardless of score.

Decision discipline

  • Continue: promising result with acceptable quality and risk; replicate or expand carefully.
  • Modify: partial evidence or clear design flaw; change one major variable and document.
  • Stop: poor fit, harmful side effects, excessive cost or evidence against the hypothesis.
  • Inconclusive: insufficient, noisy or unreliable data; do not force a winner.

Guardrails

  • Do not manufacture testimonials, customer counts, conversion results or benchmark claims.
  • Do not use personal or sensitive data as targeting inputs without an appropriate lawful basis and controls.
  • Respect community rules, platform restrictions, consent and opt-outs.
  • Require owner approval for spend, external publication, direct messaging or consequential product changes.
  • Record actual outcomes and evidence links; never represent worksheet scores as forecasted revenue.
