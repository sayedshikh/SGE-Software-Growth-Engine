Growth measurement

Measurement & attribution system

A practical, transparent way to connect campaigns with measurable visits, inquiries and verified sales—without treating clicks, intent or modeled attribution as revenue.

1. Measurement hierarchy

Reach

Impressions or views reported by the channel. Record platform, date range and whether metric is unique or total.

Visit

Landing-page sessions or clicks. Clicks and sessions are different measures and may not match.

Engagement

Useful page actions: tool use, outbound seller-page click, download or meaningful content interaction. Define each event before counting.

Lead / intent

Qualified inquiry, voluntarily supplied contact, product question or another documented intent signal. Do not count anonymous page views as leads.

Purchase

Only seller/payment evidence or an owner-confirmed transaction counts as a sale. Deduplicate and record refunds or cancellations separately.

2. Minimum campaign record

Use one campaign identifier consistently across link tags, campaign notes and outcome records. Suggested fields:

  • Campaign ID; product/edition; channel/platform; campaign and creative name.
  • UTM source, medium, campaign, content and optional term.
  • Destination URL; start/end dates; owner; status.
  • Spend and currency (actual only); views/impressions; clicks; landing sessions.
  • Engaged sessions/actions; outbound seller-page clicks; inquiries/leads.
  • Orders, gross sales, refunds, net sales, payment fees (only when evidenced).
  • Evidence link or record reference; reporting window; attribution method; notes.

3. UTM convention

utm_source=platform_or_partner
utm_medium=community|social|directory|email|affiliate|paid
utm_campaign=product_goal_YYYYMM
utm_content=placement_or_asset_variant
utm_term=optional_keyword_or_audience

Use lowercase, stable values, no personal information in tags, and avoid inconsistent spellings. Never add UTMs to links where platform rules or privacy considerations prohibit them.

4. KPI definitions

  • CTR: clicks ÷ impressions (only when both are comparable and channel defines them).
  • Visit rate: sessions ÷ clicks; may differ due to blocking, timing and platform measurement.
  • Lead rate: recorded leads ÷ sessions.
  • Purchase conversion: evidenced purchases ÷ sessions or leads; clearly label denominator and period.
  • Net revenue: evidenced gross sales minus refunds/chargebacks; fees and tax handling must be stated separately.
  • ROAS: attributed revenue ÷ ad spend. Only for campaigns with actual spend and defensible revenue attribution.
  • ROI: (attributed profit − campaign spend) ÷ campaign spend is not interchangeable with ROAS. Define whether product costs, fees and refunds are included.

5. Attribution limits

  • Use a documented model (first-touch, last-touch or assisted); do not switch models without noting it.
  • Marketplace checkout may not preserve UTMs or expose buyer-level source data. In that case, report link clicks and seller-reported outcomes separately.
  • Attribution is evidence-informed, not causal proof. Direct, organic, cross-device and delayed purchases can complicate source assignment.
  • Use aggregate reporting; minimize personal data and follow applicable consent, privacy and platform rules.

7. Reporting cadence

  • Weekly: check link integrity, campaign activity, evidence quality and anomalies; avoid premature conclusions.
  • Monthly: compare channels by qualified outcomes, effort, costs and product fit; pause low-quality or unsafe placements.
  • At experiment end: record hypothesis, actual observations, limitations, decision and next step.
