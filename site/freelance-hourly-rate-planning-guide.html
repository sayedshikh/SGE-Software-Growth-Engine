How to estimate a freelance hourly rate without guessing

A rate is a planning input—not simply the number of hours you work multiplied by a target salary. Account for billable time, operating costs and the assumptions behind your estimate.

1. Separate working time from billable time

Client work is only part of freelance work. Administration, prospecting, meetings, revisions, learning and downtime can reduce the hours you can realistically invoice. Use a conservative billable-hours estimate rather than assuming every working hour is paid.

2. Add your target income and operating costs

Include your income goal and the costs required to operate your business. Keep recurring and project-specific costs distinct when possible, so you can review your assumptions periodically.

3. Make reserves explicit

A reserve percentage may be used as a planning assumption for taxes, unpaid time, uncertainty or other needs. It is not a tax recommendation: tax and reserve requirements vary by location and situation.

4. Review project economics as well as your rate

Estimate total hours, direct costs, revisions and allocated overhead for each project. A project that appears attractive at its quoted fee may produce a different effective hourly result after the work is complete.

Review and adjust

  • Compare estimates with recorded hours and actual costs.
  • Track unpaid revisions and administrative work.
  • Review your pricing when scope, demand, experience or operating costs change.
  • Communicate scope and revision limits clearly before work begins.
